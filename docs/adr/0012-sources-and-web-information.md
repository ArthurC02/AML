# ADR-0012: Sources, web information and the news digest

- Status: Accepted. The three external sources (a received URL, grounding search, court judgments) and web identity evidence are decided but not built yet ([external sources](../feature/external-sources.md)); until they are, the [news digest](../feature/watchlist-and-adverse-information.md#news-digest) and the [news models](../feature/watchlist-and-adverse-information.md#news-models) read only the news attached to a Profile. The news models feed no decision; the two stage models, when configured, vote with the rules on the legal stage ([three readers](../feature/watchlist-and-adverse-information.md#three-readers)); of the rule readings, the age, birth date and gender written next to the Profile person's name in attached news are identity evidence.
- Date: 2026-11-02; updated 2026-11-10 (the small models read attached news beside the rules), 2026-11-19 (birth date and gender in attached news are identity evidence), 2026-11-20 (a person-stage classifier and a large language model vote with the rules on the legal stage; the stage rules reduced to those the vote needs), 2026-11-21 (only a unanimous vote decides the stage), 2026-11-23 (the wanted-notice stage rule deleted, measured under the unanimous vote; the vote measured on persons who are not suspects; a model that gives no answer is named on the condition; one language-model budget per analysis of a Case; a missing model answer gives UNKNOWN, never the rules' stage, and a batch job retries it before writing when the condition is raised), 2026-11-26 (grounding search may send the customer's name to the search service; three external sources and their build order; the stage vote is a rule over the readers' readings), 2026-11-27 (stated birth dates and ages more than two years off conclude NO_MATCH, on a received page and in attached news; a page's update date stands in for an unstated publication date, shown only)

## Context

PoC input is supplied files. Later the bank may integrate by API, and data may need to be crawled.

Reviewers read the news and judgments about a listed person to learn who is involved, in what role and what happened: searched, detained, indicted, convicted. The same pages often settle identity: an age, a title, a place or a court case that fits or does not fit the customer. A Profile carries at most one attached article today, and several reports of one event are common. Results must be reproducible, explainable and run inside the bank. A large language model is not excluded; whether one is used is decided by measurements on the annotated documents. Measured per person on eight annotated sets (1,733 persons), the rules give the stage of 1,567, a person-stage classifier (bert-base-chinese fine-tuned on document and person) 1,439, and Gemma 4 12B (one call per document) 1,589; each is right where the others fail often enough that a two-of-three vote (the first form of the decision below) gives 1,617, and where all three agree they are right for 0.967 or more of the persons on every measured set. On the held-back report set that vote gives 335 of 362 where the rules give 295; on the held-back court releases 137 of 148 where the rules give 140. Taken only where all three agree, the stage is right for 220 of 223 and 111 of 113 persons there (with the rules as of 2026-11-23). For persons who are not suspects (officials, victims, witnesses; 924 in the report development sets) the rules alone give a stage to 358, all three together to 20 of 924, each ALLEGED. Models come under clean licences ([ADR-0015](0015-licences.md)). Customer data stays inside the bank ([security and privacy](../feature/security-and-privacy.md)), except where a decision below lets it out.

The system must match the KYC customer against three external sources besides the Sanction / PEP lists (user, 2026-11-26): court judgments fetched periodically from the Judicial Yuan open data; a news page whose URL is received; and a grounding search, where keyword combinations built from the customer's name are searched and the pages found are read. A search for the customer can only be run with the customer's name.

## Decision

- **Readers and ports.** Customer and list data come through the Infra readers (`Txt/`, `Html/`) into Core records; a page or judgment comes through `Infra/Web` into the same records. Another acquisition path is a new Infra implementation (the search service behind a Core port); normalization and adjudication do not change.
- **Three external sources, one page path** ([external sources](../feature/external-sources.md)). A received URL, a grounding-search result and a judgment are each read into persons with their attributes and events, each person a source Profile in its own namespace (`WEB:`, `JY:`), matched against the customer like a list Profile. Build order (D3): the received URL first; grounding search reuses its page path; judgments last.
- **Grounding search may send the customer's name** (D1, 2026-11-26). The query to the search service holds the screened subject's name forms (as written, romanized) combined with fixed keyword lists, and nothing else: never a date of birth, document or other identifier, address, phone, email, account, employer, `subjectRef` or Case id. Adding an employer, a city or another attribute to the queries is a planned extension, to be supplemented later: not built, and needing its own decision on what leaves. Only the search service receives a query; fetching a result page sends no customer data. Each query is stored with its Case, never in general logs ([external lookups](../feature/security-and-privacy.md#external-lookups)). Following a Profile's own names, aliases and source URLs to the web from the list side is allowed (public list data) but not built.
- **Reading.** Pages are read by rules and the small models into people, roles and events with their dates, agencies, offences, amounts and penalties, and into identity facts about the listed person (age or birth year, title, place, nationality). Every fact keeps its sentence and source.
- **Rules and models side by side.** The rule digest and the models' readout are kept apart in the digest, each with its own provenance; neither overrides the other. The models' known errors are corrected by fixed rules only where a correction measurably helped on the annotated documents, and a rule result is not merged into the models' (on judgments and press releases it made them worse). The models are optional: without them the rules read alone.
- **Web facts are identity evidence.** A fact that fits or contradicts the customer becomes an evidence group with its citation `[S#]`, scored as a rule item weighted by its source's reliability ([scoring rule](../feature/scoring-rule.md)). Until a Golden Case validates it, web evidence cannot close a pair as NO_MATCH or raise it above PROBABLE_MATCH; it supports, conflicts and orders review. The same holds for attached news, where it is built: an age, birth date or gender that rules read next to the Profile person's name is compared with the customer's. Such a value weighs less than the Profile's own field (a full birth date is `MEDIUM`, not `HIGH`), does not count twice when the Profile says the same, and is a conflict only when the Profile's own field does not agree with the customer. One exception to "cannot close" is decided (user, 2026-11-27, [ADR-0001](0001-identity-adjudication.md)), for a received page and attached news alike: when every stated birth date and dated age is more than two years from the customer's DOB and the Profile has no DOB of its own, the pair is NO_MATCH (`STATED_DOB_CONFLICT`). Accepted and not measured: it can close the same person when a text states an age as of a past event without 時年 under a later report date, or a traditionally counted age (虛歲) at the boundary is offset further; to be checked on real labelled pages and news before the rule is relied on. Only rule readings are used; a model reading is not evidence.
- **One event, one piece of evidence.** Reports of the same party, event time and charge are one event; the summary lists every source of it. A fact stated by one source is never generalised to all.
- **Every source used has a summary**: publisher, date, title, then the people and events in fixed sentence patterns, each phrase a slot of a cited sentence. Rewording never swaps actor and object, and the event date is never the publication date. A page's date is the publication date it states (metadata, label, dateline), or `MISSING`; only when it states none does its update date stand in (user, 2026-11-27), marked `UPDATE_DATE` and shown, never a report date: a site's update date was measured up to a year after, and another date field more than two years before, the publication date, so an age stated on such a page is not compared ([external sources](../feature/external-sources.md#common-path)).
- **Source condition, legal stage and AML relevance** keep their own rules over structured Profile fields; the digest does not set them.
- **The legal stage in attached news is a vote.** The vote is a fixed rule over three readings (D4: no model decides); two optional stage models are readers beside the rules (`IStageReader`): the person-stage classifier in the process (ONNX), and a large language model on an endpoint inside the bank (Ollama). With both answering, only a stage all three give decides; when only two agree, or none, the stage is UNKNOWN and a reviewer reads it with every reader's stage beside it (2026-11-21: all three alike were right for 97 % to 100 % of the persons on every measured set, two alike for 68 % to 95 %, so a two-to-one vote is not an automatic answer). With one model configured the rules' stage stands and a model that differs only orders review. Without models the rules read alone, as before. Every reader's stage, and the language model's quote, stays on the condition, so the reviewer sees who said what. A configured model that fails or times out gives a missing answer, as a missing input field is `MISSING` and never an invented value: the stage is UNKNOWN without a basis, never the rules' stage alone nor a "unanimous" of the readers left, and the condition carries the indicator `STAGE_READER_NO_ANSWER` with the model listed without a stage, so a missing answer is told apart from an answer UNKNOWN and from a model not configured. An answer that follows the schema but gives the person no stage is UNKNOWN, a vote. It never blocks the analysis: a batch job with attempts left writes nothing and retries (`STAGE_NO_ANSWER`) when the answer is missing on a raised condition (HIT or UNKNOWN; a NOT_HIT condition keeps the indicator and is no reason to retry), its last attempt writes the revision with the indicator; the synchronous API writes at once (2026-11-23: the rules alone give a stage to about 4 in 10 persons who are not suspects, so a model outage must not hand the stage back to them).
- **The stage rules are only those the vote needs** (2026-11-20): a rule whose removal kept the three-way vote above 0.90 (or not below its baseline) on every measured set was deleted, so the rules alone read the stage less well than before ([stage from attached news](../feature/watchlist-and-adverse-information.md#stage-from-attached-news)).

## Consequences

- The news digest, its codes and its known limits are specified in the [feature spec](../feature/watchlist-and-adverse-information.md#news-digest); web identity evidence will add evidence groups and codes there and in [identity adjudication](../feature/identity-adjudication.md).
- A grounding-search query carries the customer's name to the search service: personal data leaves the bank there and only there, so the provider's terms on keeping queries are part of choosing it ([open question](../plan/open-questions.md#external-sources)). List-side queries, once built, carry public list data only. Each query is recorded per Case.
- Model accuracy is measured on the bank's annotated judgments and press releases, outside the repository, and recorded in the feature spec; there is no Golden Case for web evidence yet.
- With the stage models configured, a revision depends on two models and the language model's answer: temperature 0 and a fixed seed make it repeatable on one server and model build, not across builds. The condition names each reader that voted, not its model version.
- The language model's call takes seconds to minutes inside the synchronous analysis, one per hit with attached news. One call's timeout (`StageModels:LlmTimeoutSeconds`, default 60 s) and the budget of all calls in one analysis of a Case (`StageModels:LlmBudgetSeconds`, default 90 s, at least the timeout) stay below the batch deadline (`Batch:Timeout`, 2 minutes), so a slow server leaves a Case's language-model answers missing, never its revision: the hits past the budget are not asked, their stage is UNKNOWN with `STAGE_READER_NO_ANSWER`, and a batch job retries first.
- Whether the language model may be used at all is open under [ADR-0015](0015-licences.md): it stays off unless configured.
